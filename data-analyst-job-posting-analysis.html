Section 1.0

Introduction

This analysis examines 400 data analyst job postings to explore the relationship between required experience, programming language skills, and salary expectations. The dataset includes information on median salary estimates, minimum years of experience needed, and programming language requirements — R, Python, both, or neither.

Julius AI was used to assist with the analysis, allowing patterns in the data to surface efficiently while critical thinking guided the interpretation of results.

Section 2.0

Years of Experience Analysis

2.1  Minimum Years Experience vs. Median Salary Estimate

The analysis reveals a weak relationship between years of experience and median salary for data analyst positions.

r = 0.052 The correlation coefficient of 0.052 indicates virtually no linear association between minimum years of experience required and the median salary offered.

This finding is somewhat surprising — we might naturally expect higher salaries for positions requiring more experience. However, the wide spread of salaries at every experience level tells a different story.

Other factors — industry, company size, location, or specific technical skills — likely play a far more significant role in determining compensation than years of experience alone.

Section 3.0

Programming Language Analysis

3.1  Median Salary Distribution by Programming Language

A notable difference in salary distributions across programming language requirements was identified. Roles requiring proficiency in both R and Python command the highest average salary ($75,762), followed closely by Python-only positions ($74,866). Jobs requiring neither programming language have a mean salary of $70,398, while R-only positions show the lowest average at $64,750.

The findings suggest that proficiency in multiple programming languages — particularly Python, or both Python and R — is associated with higher compensation in the data analyst job market. Many roles did not explicitly list a programming language requirement at all.

The small sample size within individual language categories warrants some caution before drawing firm conclusions from specific salary figures.

Section 4.0

Conclusion

There is a weak relationship between years of experience and median salary for data analyst roles — experience alone is a poor predictor of pay.

Roles asking for both Python and R tend to offer higher average salaries than roles listing only R or neither language.

Industry, location, and company size are likely the stronger drivers of salary variation in the data analyst market.

Section 5.0

Reflection on Using Julius AI

One thing that surprised me was how quickly Julius could reveal patterns that might have taken much longer to identify manually. It was also striking to see how small changes in the way I framed a question could lead to very different insights — highlighting how important precision and clarity are in data analysis.
