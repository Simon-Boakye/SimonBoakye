Global Comparison of Malaria and Diarrhoeal Deaths in Children Aged 1–59 Months

Public Health Data Analysis Using 2020 Gapminder Data

Global Health • Child Mortality • Data Analysis

This project explores the global burden of malaria and diarrhoeal deaths among children aged 1–59 months using 2020 Gapminder data. The analysis highlights geographic disparities, identifies high-burden countries, and examines the relationship between the two leading causes of child mortality.

1. Overview

Malaria and diarrhoeal diseases remain among the leading causes of mortality among children under five years globally, particularly in low- and middle-income countries. Understanding the geographic distribution and relationship between these diseases is essential for designing targeted public health interventions.

This analysis used Gapminder data to compare malaria and diarrhoeal deaths among children aged 1–59 months across countries in 2020. The study used world maps and scatterplots to visualize patterns and identify countries with the greatest burden.

Key Finding: Countries with high malaria deaths frequently also experience high diarrhoeal deaths, suggesting overlapping structural determinants such as poverty, weak health systems, and limited access to sanitation and clean water.

2. Data and Methods

Two datasets were used in this analysis:

  • Malaria deaths among children aged 1–59 months
  • Diarrhoeal deaths among children aged 1–59 months

The datasets covered 192 countries between 2000 and 2021. However, this analysis focused specifically on the year 2020 to provide a recent global comparison.

To improve interpretability and account for skewed distributions, values were log-transformed where appropriate. World maps and scatterplots were generated to visualize disease burden and relationships between indicators.

3. Results

3.1 Malaria Deaths in Children Aged 1–59 Months

Malaria deaths were heavily concentrated in sub-Saharan Africa, where many countries continue to face major challenges related to prevention, treatment access, and healthcare infrastructure.

The countries with the highest malaria burden included Nigeria, the Democratic Republic of Congo, Niger, Mozambique, and Burkina Faso.

Figure 1. Global distribution of malaria deaths among children aged 1–59 months.

3.2 Diarrhoeal Deaths in Children Aged 1–59 Months

Diarrhoeal deaths also showed substantial concentration in sub-Saharan Africa, although major burdens were additionally observed in parts of South Asia.

Countries with the highest diarrhoeal mortality included Nigeria, India, the Democratic Republic of Congo, Pakistan, and Niger.

Figure 2. Global distribution of diarrhoeal deaths among children aged 1–59 months.

4. Relationship Between Malaria and Diarrhoeal Deaths

The scatterplot analysis revealed a moderate-to-strong positive relationship between malaria and diarrhoeal deaths among children aged 1–59 months.

Countries with higher malaria mortality also tended to experience higher diarrhoeal mortality.

Pearson Correlation: r = 0.66
Figure 3. Relationship between malaria deaths and diarrhoeal deaths among children aged 1–59 months.

5. Conclusion and Policy Recommendations

Malaria and diarrhoeal deaths frequently co-occur, particularly in sub-Saharan Africa. The findings suggest shared structural determinants, including poverty, weak healthcare systems, poor sanitation, and limited access to clean water.

Countries such as Nigeria, the Democratic Republic of Congo, Niger, Mozambique, India, and Pakistan contribute substantially to the global burden and should remain priority targets for intervention.

Policy Recommendations

  • Strengthen integrated child health programs in high-burden countries.
  • Expand access to sanitation and safe drinking water.
  • Improve malaria prevention and treatment services.
  • Invest in primary healthcare systems and maternal-child health infrastructure.
  • Increase surveillance and data-driven policy interventions.
